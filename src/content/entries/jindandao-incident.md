The Jindandao (金丹道) incident was a Han secret-society revolt across the Josotu and Juu Uda leagues in November and December 1891 that massacred Mongols; it was suppressed by late December of that year. This atlas's master chronology records its lasting consequence as political rather than only immediate: it strengthened Mongol nationalism and prompted Beijing to route Mongol-Han disputes and tax collection through Chinese provincial authorities rather than the banners themselves, weakening banner autonomy going forward. The chronology identifies it as the earliest node in this atlas's [land-reclamation](/entry/land-reclamation) thematic thread. Every date and detail here is reference-tier only, not independently citable scholarship.

**Casualty figure UNSETTLED:** estimates across sources range so widely, roughly 150,000 to 500,000, that this atlas treats the figure as genuinely contested rather than picking a number or a midpoint. This is recorded as a standing flag, not a gap to be filled by interpolation.
