Kanai Shoji (also rendered with a macron, Kanai Shōji, notably by the Hoover Institution) began his career as a physician and bacteriologist and ended it as a senior Japanese colonial administrator; his relevance to this atlas is almost entirely the second half of that career, above all his years as Supreme Advisor (最高顧問) to [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub)'s Mengjiang government, where his office, more than any single Mongol official's, held real financial, police, and transport power behind the prince's formal chairmanship. His earlier medical career and Manchukuo administrative posts are covered below as the lead-up to that role, weighted accordingly — briefly, as background — rather than as this entry's center of gravity. The frontmatter years above span his earliest and latest independently documented dates (birth in 1886; his 1947 Tokyo Tribunal testimony), not a claim that his active career filled that whole span without gaps.

**A note on his kanji:** this atlas has seen the form 金井章次 proposed for his name but has not confirmed it against any source in hand. It is NOT written into this entry as settled and should not be treated as verified until a maintainer checks it against a primary Japanese-language source — see this atlas's project notes for the standing verify-gate. Update: the kanji 金井章次 is now corroborated by two independent Japanese-language reference sources, one of which (Kotobank) attributes it to a named published biographical dictionary, 20世紀日本人名事典 (Nichigai Associates, 2004), alongside a career spine (Nagano birth 1886-12-01, Tokyo Imperial University medical faculty, Kitasato Institute, London, League of Nations Health Section, Keio professorship, South Manchuria Railway) that matches this atlas's IMTFE-derived account. The same sources also independently record the 間島省 (Chientao/Jiandao) governorship this atlas had recorded as an unresolved conflict, and give the Mengjiang supreme-advisor tenure as 1939–1941. These are reference-tier corroborations, not a primary source; per this atlas's rule the maintainer should confirm 金井章次 against 20世紀日本人名事典 or a primary Japanese-language source before the kanji is set as settled in the headword. A death date of 1967-12-03 also surfaces in these sources and is logged as a candidate to verify (distinct from this entry's endYear 1947, which marks last documented activity, not death).

## Physician, bacteriologist, and South Manchuria Railway administrator (lead-up)

The following biographical spine is drawn from the personnel record in Kanai's International Military Tribunal for the Far East (IMTFE, the postwar "Tokyo Tribunal") witness packet — the higher-reliability layer of that packet for basic career dates, as distinct from the packet's defense testimony narrative, discussed further below. This atlas states it as recorded by that record, not independently verified against any other source: born December 1, 1886, in Nagano Prefecture; graduated from the Medical Department of Tokyo Imperial University in December 1912; worked as a bacteriologist, including at the Kitazato Institute, then at the Lister Institute in London in 1920, then at the League of Nations Health Section in Geneva from January 1921; received his MD in 1922; and was appointed professor of bacteriology at Keio University in 1923.

His move into colonial administration began within the South Manchuria Railway (SMR) — a company this atlas does not yet have a dedicated entry for; see this atlas's project notes for that as a future cross-link candidate. Per the personnel record, he became Chief of the SMR's Sanitary Section in April 1924, and Chief of the South Manchurian Sanitary Research Institute from August 1926 — public-health administrative posts that sat inside the same corporate-colonial apparatus that governed much of Japan's presence in Manchuria in this period.

## From physician to colonial governor, 1931–1936

Kanai resigned from the SMR in October 1931 — the month after the Mukden Incident (September 18, 1931) that began Japan's occupation of Manchuria — to become Supreme Advisor to the Society for Maintaining Peace in Liaoning Province, based at Mukden (personnel record). This atlas does not yet have a dedicated entry for Manchukuo, the Japanese-sponsored state that followed in March 1932; see this atlas's project notes for that as a future cross-link candidate. From here his career ran through a sequence of Manchukuo provincial and investigative posts, per the same personnel record: Chief of the General Affairs Office, Mukden Province, in 1932; Director of the Special Investigation Committee on the System of Local Affairs in 1934; Chief of the General Affairs Office, Pinchiang Province, from December 1935; and Provincial Governor of Antung from July 1936.

**A conflict in the sources, recorded not resolved:** the narrative summary of Kanai's own IMTFE defense testimony states that he was governor of Chientao (Jiandao, 間島省) Province in 1935 — a posting that does not appear anywhere in the same witness packet's personnel record, which instead moves directly from the Pinchiang General Affairs post (December 1935) to the Antung governorship (July 1936) with no Jiandao posting between them. This atlas records both accounts side by side: the personnel record's silence on a Jiandao governorship may mean the narrative summary is simply mistaken, or that the personnel record omits a genuine but brief posting, or some other reconciliation this atlas cannot determine from the material in hand. Neither account is preferred here.

## Supreme Advisor to Mengjiang (the core of this entry)

**A second conflict in the sources, recorded not resolved:** three sources used in this atlas date the start of Kanai's Mengjiang advisorship differently, and none is harmonized here into a single settled date. A Chinese-language aggregator (Baidu Baike — low reliability, and framed throughout in explicitly "puppet"/"pseudo-state" terms) places him as Japanese supreme advisor from the [Mongolian Allied League Autonomous Government](/entry/mongolian-allied-league-autonomous-government)'s founding in October 1937. The IMTFE personnel record above instead lists "Supreme Advisor to the Mongolian Government, 1938." A Hoover Institution news release — a reputable institutional secondary source, logged in this atlas's project notes with its full holdings lead — dates his role as supreme political advisor to the Mengjiang government to 1939 through 1941. This atlas suspects, but cannot confirm from the sources in hand, that the divergence tracks the Mongol government's own successive reorganizations in this period (the October 1937 Mongolian Allied League Autonomous Government, the November 1937 [United Committee of Mengchiang](/entry/united-committee-of-mengchiang), and the September 1939 [Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government](/entry/mongolian-allied-autonomous-government)) rather than three sources describing one unchanging appointment — but this is offered as a plausible reading, not a resolution, and all three dates are recorded here rather than one being selected.

Within that broader span, this atlas can date one specific installation precisely: Kanai directed the September 1, 1939 founding ceremony of the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government in the courtyard of the United Committee of Mengchiang, and was installed as that government's Supreme Advisor (Jagchid). Per Boyle, Demchugdongrub had resigned that April as Director-General of the United Committee of Mengchiang in dissatisfaction with Japanese policy, and Kanai took over that role as well (Boyle, p.133) — this atlas's master chronology and the United Committee of Mengchiang entry treat this as the same office described from two different sources.

Kanai used his position to shape Mongol politics directly rather than only administratively. He promoted the appointment of Prince Sungjingwangchug as Minister of Civilian Affairs, hoping to build up a rival who could eventually replace Demchugdongrub — a plan that, per Jagchid (who became Prince Sung's secretary), backfired when Sung proved loyal and Demchugdongrub came to rely on him as a trusted intermediary with the Japanese instead (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government entry). Kanai also arranged [Wu Heling](/entry/wu-heling)'s effective exile to Japan under the pretext of deepening his knowledge of the country, removing a senior Mongol rival from Mengjiang politics; Jagchid, who accompanied Wu there from 1938 as his secretary, gives this episode in detail in the Wu Heling entry (Jagchid).

[Sechin Jagchid](/entry/sechin-jagchid) frames the broader relationship between the two men as an ongoing, losing political battle for Demchugdongrub: the concentration of Mengjiang's central institutions at [Kalgan](/entry/kalgan) from late 1937 onward "consolidated the authority of the Japanese supreme advisor Kanai Shoji at the prince's expense" (Jagchid; see the Kalgan entry).

## Decline, replacement, and departure

Kanai's dominance did not last through the whole Mengjiang period. Late in 1941, under the [Mongolian Autonomous State](/entry/mongolian-autonomous-state), he was removed and succeeded by Ohashi Chuichi, who himself went on to clash with the Japanese military over the state's autonomy (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Autonomous State entry). A visit to Kalgan that same year by the Japanese army minister Tojo Hideki, who presented Demchugdongrub with 500 rifles for a personal bodyguard, is recorded elsewhere in this atlas as a signal that Kanai no longer dominated Mongol affairs even before his formal removal (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government entry). The IMTFE personnel record dates his formal retirement precisely — "retired from office by request, December 1941" — sharpening what this atlas had previously recorded only as "late 1941."

Sechin Jagchid's memoir *The Last Mongol Prince* — written by a participant in this same milieu, not a neutral outside historian, a standing caveat this atlas applies to all of Jagchid's material — includes in its illustrations list a photograph of a farewell party held for Kanai as the outgoing Japanese Supreme Advisor, in Beijing, in early December 1941, matching the retirement date above. **Verify-gate:** this photograph and its listing surfaced via an online full text of uncertain legality; this atlas has not confirmed the exact page or the precise caption wording against a legitimate copy of the book, and that confirmation is left to the maintainer before this detail is treated as fully settled. This atlas does not reproduce any text from that source beyond this paraphrased fact of the photograph's existence and dating.

## After Mengjiang: the Race Research Institute and Tokyo Tribunal testimony

Kanai's postwar documentary trail, per the IMTFE personnel record, resumes with his appointment as Councillor of the Race Research Institute (民族研究所), a Cabinet appointment, on August 4, 1943 — a two-year gap this atlas has no source to fill.

His final documented appearance in this atlas's sources is as a defense witness before the IMTFE in April 1947, part of a witness packet held at the National Diet Library (NDL scan NDL11918515). That packet contains four distinct documents this atlas treats as four distinct voices, not one: a defense narrative summary of his direct testimony (transcript pages 19219–19232); a prosecution memorandum dated April 17, 1947; a witness progress report; and the personnel record already cited above for his career dates. **This packet's romanizations of Chinese and Japanese names are unreliable and have been mapped to this atlas's standard forms before use here** — see this atlas's project notes mapping table.

The narrative summary is exculpatory defense testimony, and this atlas treats it as such throughout, not as flat fact: it was solicited on behalf of Kanai by the Class-A defendants Itagaki Seishiro, Okawa Shumei, Minami Jiro, Umezu Yoshijiro, and Hoshino Naoki — men with a direct interest in favorable testimony from a witness who had served in the same colonial administrative apparatus. Most notably, Kanai testified — this atlas states this explicitly as testimony, not as an established fact — that opium control under his administration was conducted rigorously and had the effect of reducing smoking. This atlas holds no independent source corroborating or contradicting that specific claim and does not evaluate it here.

The prosecution's own memorandum is a separate, distinct voice from the defense narrative above, and this atlas attributes it as such: it records the prosecution's judgment that Kanai's testimony was unimportant to its case. This atlas holds no further detail from that memorandum beyond that characterization. The witness progress report, the packet's fourth document, is logged here as part of the packet's provenance; this atlas has no specific content to relay from it beyond its existence.

IMTFE tribunal records are public-domain documents, but this atlas paraphrases them rather than quoting at length, per house style.
