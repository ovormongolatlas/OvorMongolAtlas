The Abaganar (also written Abkhanar or Abaghanar) were one of the five Mongol tribes of Shilingol League, organized under the Qing as the Abaganar Left and Abaganar Right Banners [1, 2]. Like the [Abaga](/entry/abaga-banners), their princes descended from Belgütei, a brother of Chinggis Khan [1, 2].

*A note on sources.* The sources consulted say little about these banners beyond their origins and the loyalties of their rulers in the 1930s. The entry draws on [Sechin Jagchid](/entry/sechin-jagchid) [1], Christopher Atwood’s encyclopedia [2] and Isabelle Charleux’s study of the Berlin banner maps [3].

## Origins

Atwood writes that the Abaga and Abaganar were formerly the Ongni’ud, a camp district of the Chahar, whose princes revolted against Ligdan Khan in 1627 and fled north to Khalkha. After the Qing defeated Ligdan they submitted in 1637, received land in today’s Shilingol, and were renamed Abaga and Abaganar, “uncles,” for their descent from Chinggis Khan’s brother [2].

## In league politics (1930s–1945)

In 1933, when [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub) launched the autonomy movement, Sodnamnuobu, the ruling *beile* of Abaganar Right, was one of the few Shilingol rulers who backed him. Agdungga, the ruling *beile* of Abaganar Left, followed the old Abaga prince Yangsang instead, out of tradition and because his banner lay close to Abaga [1]. By April 1936 Balgunsurung was jasag of Abaganar Left; he attended the Üjümüchin conference that year and the Second Mongolian Congress at Hohhot in October 1937 [1]. In August 1945 he was head of the Civil Affairs Bureau in the Shilingol League office when the Soviet army crossed the border [1].

The two maps of Abaganar Right show how much maps of the same banner could differ. The 1901 map (cat. 818) draws nine ovoos on mountain summits, out of twenty-one mountains inside the banner and twelve on its boundaries; the 1907 map (cat. 819) draws only three, and Charleux finds that neither the date nor the language of the map explains the difference [3, n. 123]. Some of the tufts drawn on the mountain tops of cat. 818 may represent wooden ovoos [3, fig. 4b], and both maps name a Qara oboγa, “black ovoo” [3, n. 35]. The [Shilingol League](/entry/shilingol-league) entry describes the collection.

## Open questions

- **The banners’ later history.** The sources consulted are silent on the Abaganar banners after 1945.
- **Where the banner seats were.** Haltod’s *Mongolische Ortsnamen* (1966) lists the place names on these maps.
