Bai Haifeng was a Mongol officer and politician who changed sides several times between the early 1930s and 1950. Trained in the Soviet Union and sent into Inner Mongolia as a Communist, he became a member of the Kuomintang, served as security chief under [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub) at Bailingmiao, turned against him, and became a lieutenant general under the Nationalist commander Hu Zongnan. In 1949 he sat on Demchugdongrub’s committee at Alashan. In 1950 he came to Demchugdongrub as an envoy of the Chinese Communists.

*A note on sources.* Almost everything here comes from four US intelligence reports of 1949–50 (numbers 1, 3, 4 and 5 below), which are unevaluated informant reporting. The fullest [1] warns that its informants were hostile to him. Where [Sechin Jagchid](/entry/sechin-jagchid)’s memoir of Demchugdongrub overlaps, it is cited as [2]. No other scholarly source used in this atlas treats Bai Haifeng directly. Christopher P. Atwood’s *Young Mongols and Vigilantes in Inner Mongolia’s Interregnum Decades, 1911–1931* (Brill, 2002) does, and the atlas holds it but has not yet consulted it for this entry. **Verify-gate:** consult Atwood 2002 and rebuild the sourcing here before this account is treated as settled.

## Early career

Bai Haifeng studied at a Far Eastern university in the Soviet Union and joined the Communist Party. When the Third International sent him into Inner Mongolia, he switched allegiance to the Kuomintang party [1].

In the mid-1930s he was chief of the Peace Preservation Section under Demchugdongrub’s [Bailingmiao regime](/entry/mongolian-political-affairs-council), the Bat-khaalag council. He ordered the Blue Shirts Society of the KMT to assassinate Han Fenglin, a pro-Japanese officer on Demchugdongrub’s staff, and was placed under surveillance for it [1]. He then took part in a rebellion based at Bailingmiao. Sechin Jagchid mentions it in passing: the official Jakhunju followed Bai Haifeng, Yun Jixian and most of the Peace Preservation Troops in it [2]. This is one of the few points where a CIA informant account and an independent participant source agree.

He fled to the Chinese general Fu Zuoyi, urged Demchugdongrub’s Peace Preservation Corps to defect, and raised a Mongol cavalry unit that saw no action. After a break with Fu he joined Hu Zongnan, commanding a cavalry division and later a brigade with the rank of lieutenant general. He left the army in 1948 after falling out with Hu [1].

## The 1948 National Assembly

In the 1948 National Assembly he helped defeat a Mongolian-autonomy bill, and gave the Central Government a list of the names of Mongolian nationalists [1]. According to Sechin Jagchid, the [Mongolian Youth Alliance](/entry/mongolian-youth-alliance) refused to sign a petition circulated to expand Bai Haifeng’s own military forces, and his bloc was outvoted when the alliance pushed constitutional amendments over his objections [2]. See that entry for the fuller episode.

## The Northwest, 1949

As second-in-command of Fan Hanjie’s army group he tried, without success, to raise a unit in Northwest China [1]. Report 1 says he went to the Northwest to prevent Mongolian autonomy and to seize the troops of the Mongol commander Wu Guting (Mongol name Ogodei, rendered “Ukudui” in these reports), and that he was pushing Prince Ta of Alashan (Damzana) to oppose Demchugdongrub [1].

In March 1949 a rumor spread that Sühbaatar, acting brigade commander of Wu Guting’s units, had been assassinated. Mongols returning from Shanba said Bai Haifeng had put the story about to seize the troops, and that Sühbaatar was alive and in command. The report calls Bai Haifeng “not very highly trusted” [3].

Another report of April 1949 lists him among the leaders who “agreed to unite and defend the area against the Chinese Communists,” with Demchugdongrub and Prince Ta, and places him in the January 1949 discussions at Lanzhou [4]. In August 1949 he sat on the Peace Preservation Committee of Demchugdongrub’s [Alashan government](/entry/mongolian-autonomous-government-alashan), which that entry treats in full. At Alashan, according to Jagchid, he sided with [Wu Heling](/entry/wu-heling)’s realist bloc against Demchugdongrub’s plan for a government in exile in Tibet, and argued for staying and working out a plan to remain in Mongolia [2]. Wu Heling talked Damzana and He Zhaolin out of the exile plan in the same debate; see Wu Heling’s entry.

## 1950: envoy to Demchugdongrub

A September 1950 report describes Bai Haifeng, now a member of the Communist Northwest Commission, being sent in May 1950 with He Zhaolin by the Chinese Communists’ Northwest Military and Political Commission to persuade Demchugdongrub to join them. The two did not manage to see him and were said to be making a second attempt [5].

## Two portraits, one man

The sources give two portraits that do not fit together. In report 1, Bai Haifeng is a consistent opponent of Mongol autonomy who denounced nationalists and tried to take other men’s troops. In report 4 and in Jagchid’s account, he sits beside Demchugdongrub at Alashan in 1949 and argues for staying. By 1950 he is carrying the Communists’ offer to the same prince. The sources record this trajectory without explaining it, and this entry leaves it open.

Part of the contradiction may come from the informants. Report 1 says its sources were “strongly opposed to the CC Clique” and that its material “may be colored by their attitude.” That warning applies to every judgment in it, and troop numbers in all four CIA reports are estimates, not counts.

Report 1 gives a fuller portrait of Prince Ta of Alashan, Damzana (also Darijaya / Dali Zhai): wealthy, Manchu-intermarried, raised in Beijing, and reportedly not fluent in Mongolian. It belongs with the [Alashan government](/entry/mongolian-autonomous-government-alashan) entry, where he is a central figure.
