The Khuuchid (Khauchid, Qaγučid; Chinese Haoqi) were one of the five Mongol tribes of Shilingol League, organized under the Qing as the Khuuchid Left and Khuuchid Right Banners [1, 2]. Their princes, like those of the Sünid and Üjümüchin, descended from Torubolod, eldest son of Dayan Khan [1]. After 1945 the two banners were merged into Üjümüchin [2].

*A note on sources.* The entry draws on [Sechin Jagchid](/entry/sechin-jagchid) [1], Christopher Atwood’s encyclopedia [2], Tachibana Makoto’s archive-based study of Shilingol in 1912–1913 [3] and Isabelle Charleux’s study of the Berlin banner maps [4].

## Origins

When Ligdan Khan allied with the Ming against the Manchus, the Khuuchid, Sünid and Üjümüchin moved north to Khalkha rather than fight him, and later came back and submitted to the Manchus [1]. Jagchid dates the Khuuchid submission to 1651, seven years after the Manchus took Beijing [1]. Atwood dates the submission of the Sünid, Üjümüchin and Abaga princes to 1637 and does not give a separate date for the Khuuchid [2]. Khuuchid Right was a small banner, with five of the league’s 113 *sumu* [1].

## Under the Bogd Khan and the Republic (1912–1945)

In October 1912, when Urga’s troops seized the league head Yangsang, the two Khuuchid banners were the only ones in Shilingol that had already declared for the Bogd Khan [3]; by January 1913 all ten had followed [3]. The [Five Roads campaign](/entry/five-roads-campaign) entry follows the war.

In 1933 the Khuuchid rulers, Sangdagdorji of Khuuchid Right and Sungjingwangchug of Khuuchid Left, were faithful followers of the league head, Sodnamrabdan of [Üjümüchin](/entry/ujumuchin) Right rather than of [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub), whose autonomy movement had few backers in the league [1]. Both attended the Üjümüchin conference of April 1936 [1]. By the Second Mongolian Congress at Hohhot in October 1937 Sungjingwangchug was deputy head of the league [1], and in August 1945 he was its head, at home in Khuuchid Left Banner when the Soviet army crossed the border [1].

Three of the four (cats. 812–814) are drawn on a measuring grid, which few Inner Mongolian banner maps use [4, n. 26]. The 1901 map of Khuuchid Left (cat. 812) names a flat-topped “Dösi aγula,” “anvil mountain”; such mountains, Charleux notes, were said to have been flattened when Chinggis Khan or another giant hero forged weapons on them [4, n. 119]. The 1901 map of Khuuchid Right (cat. 814) names a Čaγan oboγa, “white ovoo” [4, n. 35], and crowns its mountains with different kinds of ovoo, a mound with a tuft and pole beside a simple cairn [4]. The [Shilingol League](/entry/shilingol-league) entry describes the collection.
