The East Mongolian People’s Autonomous Government (东蒙古人民自治政府) was the eastern counterpart of the short-lived [Inner Mongolian People’s Republic](/entry/inner-mongolian-republican-provisional-government) proclaimed in Sünid Right Banner in September 1945. It was a separate body with its own leaders, based in the former Manchukuo Xing’an province around Wangyemiao (today Ulanhot), and it lasted from January to May 1946.

Most of the detail below comes from the Chinese Wikipedia article on the government, which cites Husel Borjigin’s study of Communist and Nationalist policy toward Inner Mongolia, articles in Chinese journals and local histories (Chinese Wikipedia). It is a tertiary source and the atlas has not checked its citations, so its facts are attributed to it throughout. Two contemporary American sources, a State Department memorandum and a CIA field report, give an outside view.

## From union to autonomy, 1945

On 18 August 1945, after Manchukuo collapsed, Mongol revolutionaries, young intellectuals, officials of the Xing’an provincial administration and officers of the Xing’an Army who had turned against the Japanese met at Wangyemiao. They revived the Inner Mongolian People’s Revolutionary Party with an eastern branch, and formed an Inner Mongolian People’s Liberation Committee, which declared for the merger of Inner Mongolia with the Mongolian People’s Republic. Boyanmandu, a former Manchukuo provincial governor, was chosen as its representative (Chinese Wikipedia).

In October 1945 a delegation under Boyanmandu and [Khafungga](/entry/khafungga) went to Ulaanbaatar to ask for the merger and was refused (Chinese Wikipedia). Nakami Tatsuo records the same appeal from Khafungga’s side: Choibalsan rejected it, and Khafungga’s aim turned to autonomy for Inner Mongolia alone (Nakami, cited in the Khafungga entry). A separate delegation from Hulunbuir was also turned away. A US memorandum of 1947 confirms the sequence from outside: “These Mongols had earlier moved for complete independence from China and union with Outer Mongolia” (Sprouse).

In November 1945 a delegation under Wulitu (乌力图) attended a Northeast People’s Congress in Shenyang, where the Communist official Lin Feng told them that Inner and Outer Mongolia could not merge and advised them to work with Yunze (Ulanhu) for autonomy within China. A preparatory meeting at Wangyemiao on 9 December 1945 began planning a government (Chinese Wikipedia).

## The government, January–May 1946

The East Mongolian People’s Congress opened at Gegeen Süme (葛根庙) on 16 January 1946, with representatives of 36 banners and a Communist observer, Hu Bingquan. It adopted a programme and an autonomy law, and on 19 January it issued the government’s founding declaration (Chinese Wikipedia). The [Khafungga](/entry/khafungga) entry’s family biography instead has Asigen helping to found the government at Wangyemiao in February 1946; the January date and Gegeen Süme are preferred here only because Chinese Wikipedia gives them with more detail, and the conflict is recorded.

**Officers.** Boyanmandu was chairman, Manibadar vice-chairman and Khafungga secretary-general. Asigen was minister of internal defence and commanded its troops; Temürbagen was minister of economy; and Sangjiyabu headed propaganda. The government had a committee of 15 and a “small Khural” of 45 (Chinese Wikipedia).

**Army.** The Xing’an Army was reorganised as the East Mongolian People’s Autonomous Army, with at least two cavalry divisions (Chinese Wikipedia).

**Provinces.** Its provinces were Xing’an, Jirim, Juu Uda, Josotu, Hulunbuir and Nawen Muren, the last created in March 1946 with its seat at Zalantun. Chinese Wikipedia names governors for Xing’an, Jirim, Hulunbuir and Nawen Muren, and none for Juu Uda or Josotu.

**Aims.** Chinese Wikipedia describes its goal as a high degree of autonomy for eastern Inner Mongolia, and quotes a February 1946 statement seeking “high regional autonomy within the Republic of China.” The US consulate at Changchun read its laws more sharply. Summarising the consulate’s report of 11 July 1947, Philip Sprouse wrote that the laws were “based on the concept of autonomous rule under Chinese sovereignty” but that their character “savors more of independence than of local autonomy,” and that there was “no known sanction from the Chinese Government” for the proclamation (Sprouse).

## Between the Nationalists and the Communists

**The Nationalists.** In February 1946 a seven-member delegation under Manibadar and Sangjiyabu went from Wangyemiao through Changchun to Beiping to ask for recognition of East Mongolian autonomy. On 22 February Xiong Shihui, head of the Nationalist Northeast Headquarters, received them and rejected their call for “unified Mongolian high autonomy,” and they were refused an audience with Chiang Kai-shek (Chinese Wikipedia).

**The Communists.** On 25 January 1946, at Zhengjiatun, Asigen and the Communist commander Lü Zhengcao signed an agreement on shared administration, joint operations against bandits and Mongol autonomy in Mongol areas. A second agreement in late February placed the East Mongolian army under the Communist Western Manchuria Military Region, with Communist political officers (Chinese Wikipedia).

**Chengde.** After a preparatory meeting at Chengde from 30 March to 2 April 1946, the Inner Mongolian Autonomy Movement Unification Meeting of 3 April declared for equal autonomy under Communist leadership, made the Communist-led Inner Mongolian Autonomy Movement Federation the single leading body, and decided to dissolve the East Mongolian government. A provisional congress at Wangyemiao on 26 May 1946 issued a declaration the next day abolishing the government and forming the Federation’s East Mongolian branch, with Khafungga as its chairman (Chinese Wikipedia).

## Afterwards

The East Mongolian branch carried on the government’s work until the Inner Mongolian Autonomous Government was founded in May 1947. Manibadar was arrested in September 1946 and removed from his posts in October. Nationalist troops took Tongliao in October 1946 and later Kailu, bringing much of Jirim under Nationalist control (Chinese Wikipedia).

A CIA field report of May 1947 notes that “the political nucleus of the Eastern Inner Mongolians” had moved from Wangyemiao to Hailar the previous summer, and reads the move as a pull toward the Mongolian People’s Republic and the USSR: since the Communists held most Mongol areas, the report argues, accepting Chinese guidance meant accepting communism in any case (Source E). That reading is the report’s own and is unconfirmed.

Three Mongol representatives who met American officials in Nanjing on 10 August 1947 named the “Eastern Mongolian Autonomous Government” in western Manchuria as one of two semi-autonomous bodies the Mongols had organised after the war, the other being the Democratic Liberation Committee in western Inner Mongolia (Sprouse, August 1947).

## On the Timeline Map

The government is drawn on the 1946 map as a hatched claim, although it was dissolved before 1 July. The outline is the banner lands of the Jirim, Juu Uda and Josotu leagues as a 1930s English map of the banners draws them, with Hulunbuir on its 1912 outline. Nawen Muren, around Zalantun, has no outline in hand and is not drawn. Hulunbuir also had its own autonomous provincial government in 1945–1946, which is not drawn separately.

See also [Ulanhu](/entry/ulanhu), who pressured it into accepting Communist leadership in 1946.
