Operation Chahar is the name used here for the Japanese advance of August to October 1937 through Chahar, northern Shanxi and Suiyuan, which began after the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 7 July and ended with the occupation of Baotou in mid-October. It is distinct from the “Chahar operation” of December 1935, in which Li Shouxin's force of about 5,000 took the area north of Zhangjiakou (Boyd), and that earlier operation is treated as background in the [Suiyuan campaign](/entry/suiyuan-campaign-1936) entry. Sechin Jagchid, the main source for the Mongol side, writes as a partisan of [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub) and gives the Mongol army a larger part than Boyle's Japanese-army-centred account allows; the two are set side by side below.

## Opening moves, August 1937

After the Marco Polo Bridge Incident, Jagchid says, the Japanese occupied Beiping on 7 August and a Soviet-Japanese nonaggression pact is mentioned in his account (pp. 167 to 168). Fu Zuoyi shifted to the offensive and sent a force under a commander Jagchid calls “Dong Biwu” against Shangdu, a spelling kept here as Jagchid gives it. The Mongol First Division under Yin Baoshan was defeated, the battalion under Jing Dequan surrendered, and Shi Yushan joined the Chinese side and headed for [Jabsar](/entry/jabsar). Itagaki advanced along the Beiping-Suiyuan railway toward Nankou, and the Kwantung Army airlifted some Mongol forces to Zhangbei (pp. 167 to 169).

On 16 August Demchugdongrub ordered a retreat to Doloonnor after negotiating with Colonel Kawazaki. Part of the army and the staff were evacuated; Demchugdongrub himself fled to Buyandalai's residence in Chahar and lost contact with his staff. Tojo Hideki arrived at Doloonnor and ordered Kawazaki and [Wu Heling](/entry/wu-heling) to find him (p. 169).

## The advance, late August to October

On 27 August Tojo's forces advanced from Doloonnor and Zhangbei against Chinese-occupied [Kalgan](/entry/kalgan) from the north while Itagaki approached from the south after Beiping and Nankou. Liu Ruming's army retreated and Kalgan fell (Jagchid, p. 169). The Japanese took Datong on 13 September (Jagchid). [Li Shouxin](/entry/li-shouxin) headed for Pingdiquan (Jining) and Demchugdongrub for Beile-yin sume, which Jagchid says was “liberated” by 30 September 1937. Hohhot was occupied jointly on 14 October (Jagchid, pp. 169 to 171).

Boyle describes the same advance from the Japanese side: Kwantung Army and North China Area Army elements with Mongol detachments overwhelmed Fu Zuoyi's provincial troops, crossed the Great Wall at Fengchen on 17 September and reached the Baotou railway terminus exactly one month later, with nominal control of all Inner Mongolia except western Ningxia by the end of October (pp. 124 to 128). He adds that Japanese estimates put the Mongolians in the South Chahar area at no more than 300 and in North Shanxi at “twenty-odd,” and that the Kwantung Army, after Chinese pressure, refused to let Mongol troops occupy Kalgan (pp. 124 to 128).

A Japanese army guide, *Guide to the Battlefields of the China Incident*, volume 1 (1 September 1938), gives a dated sequence for the western end of the advance, in a propagandistic register: strategic points occupied on 19 September; a mechanized force into Pingdiquan on 24 September; Taolin occupied on 29 September; a detached force capturing [Bailingmiao](/entry/bailingmiao) on 30 September; three columns attacking on 8 October; the enemy near Baotou annihilated and the flag raised that night on 14 October; and the railway terminus fully occupied on 17 October (JACAR Ref. C11110553900, read in translation). It says the Inner Mongolian forces had been pressed back north of Zhangbei a month earlier and then recovered.

## Local regimes

Jagchid lists the local administrations the advance produced. The Committee for Peace Maintenance in Kalgan (Yu Pinqing, Du Yunyu) became the [South Chahar Autonomous Government](/entry/south-chahar-autonomous-government) on 4 September, with Takagi Ichinari as adviser. In northern Shanxi the [North Shansi Autonomous Government](/entry/north-shansi-autonomous-government) was formed by Xia Gong, Ma Yongkui and Tian Rubi, with Maejima Noboru as adviser (founded 15 October at Datong, Boyle). At Hohhot a committee formed under He Bingwen. Anzai Kinji was Japanese adviser to the Chahar League, Xu Ronghuo was Wuchuan magistrate, and [Kanai Shoji](/entry/kanai-shoji) arrived as supreme adviser (Jagchid, pp. 170 to 171). The [Mongolian Allied League Autonomous Government](/entry/mongolian-allied-league-autonomous-government) followed, incorporated on 29 October after a convocation on 27 and 28 October and headed by Prince Yun (Boyle). The three governments were later joined in [Mengjiang](/entry/mengjiang).

Atwood summarizes the effect: the Japanese invasion of North China in August 1937 drove Fu Zuoyi west and delivered most of central and western Inner Mongolia to Demchugdongrub's government, and an autonomous government was set up at Hohhot in October 1937 (“Demchungdongrub, Prince”).

## Where the sources disagree

- **The fall of Baotou and Hohhot.** Jagchid gives 14 October for the joint occupation of Hohhot. The Japanese guide gives 14 October for the flag over Baotou and 17 October for the terminus. Boyle gives 17 October for Baotou (“Paotow”). Hohhot and Baotou are different cities, so these may not conflict, but only Jagchid's sentence mentions Hohhot, and nothing consulted gives a separate date for it.
- **Beile-yin sume.** Jagchid and the Japanese guide both date its recapture to 30 September, but credit different actors: Mongol forces under Demchugdongrub in Jagchid, a detached Japanese force in the guide.
- **The founding of the federation.** Boyle distinguishes a convocation on 27 and 28 October from incorporation on 29 October, so a single “founding date” of 27, 28 or 29 October depends on which step is meant. No other source consulted here dates it.
- **Mongol role.** Jagchid presents the Mongol army and Demchugdongrub as active participants in the recovery of Chahar and Suiyuan; Boyle and the Japanese guide present a Japanese-led operation with minor Mongol detachments.
