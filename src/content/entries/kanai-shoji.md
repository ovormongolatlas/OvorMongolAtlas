Kanai Shoji (rendered Kanai Shōji, with a macron, by the Hoover Institution among others) began his career as a physician and bacteriologist and ended it as a senior Japanese colonial administrator. His relevance to this atlas lies almost entirely in the second half of that career, above all his years as Supreme Advisor (最高顧問) to [Demchugdongrub](/entry/demchugdongrub)'s Mengjiang government, where his office held more real financial, police, and transport power than any single Mongol official's, behind the prince's formal chairmanship. The medical career and the Manchukuo posts are covered below briefly, as background to that role rather than as this entry's center of gravity. The frontmatter years span his earliest and latest independently documented dates, his birth in 1886 and his Tokyo Tribunal testimony in 1947; they are not a claim that his active career filled that span without gaps.

**A note on his kanji.** The form 金井章次 is corroborated by two independent Japanese-language reference sources, one of which (Kotobank) attributes it to a named published biographical dictionary, 20世紀日本人名事典 (Nichigai Associates, 2004). Those sources also carry a career spine (Nagano birth 1886-12-01, Tokyo Imperial University medical faculty, Kitasato Institute, London, League of Nations Health Section, Keio professorship, South Manchuria Railway) that matches this atlas's IMTFE-derived account independently, and they record both the 間島省 (Chientao/Jiandao) governorship this entry records below as an unresolved conflict and a Mengjiang supreme-advisor tenure of 1939 to 1941. These are reference-tier corroborations rather than a primary source, so per this atlas's rules the kanji is not yet written into the headword as settled: a maintainer should confirm it against 20世紀日本人名事典 itself or another primary Japanese-language source first. **Verify-gate:** that confirmation. A death date of 1967-12-03 also surfaces in these sources and is logged as a candidate to verify, distinct from this entry's endYear of 1947, which marks last documented activity rather than death.

## Physician, bacteriologist, and South Manchuria Railway administrator (lead-up)

The biographical spine below comes from the personnel record in Kanai's witness packet for the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (IMTFE, the postwar "Tokyo Tribunal"). Within that packet, the personnel record is the more reliable layer for basic career dates, as distinct from the defense testimony discussed further below; this entry relies on it as recorded, without independent verification against any other source. Per that record, Kanai was born on December 1, 1886, in Nagano Prefecture, and graduated from the Medical Department of Tokyo Imperial University in December 1912. He then worked as a bacteriologist: at the Kitazato Institute, at the Lister Institute in London in 1920, and at the League of Nations Health Section in Geneva from January 1921. He received his MD in 1922 and was appointed professor of bacteriology at Keio University in 1923.

His move into colonial administration began within the South Manchuria Railway (SMR), a company for which this atlas does not yet have a dedicated entry (see the project notes for that future cross-link candidate). Per the personnel record, he became Chief of the SMR's Sanitary Section in April 1924 and Chief of the South Manchurian Sanitary Research Institute in August 1926. These were public-health posts, but they sat inside the corporate-colonial apparatus that governed much of Japan's presence in Manchuria in this period.

## From physician to colonial governor, 1931–1936

Kanai resigned from the SMR in October 1931, the month after the Mukden Incident of September 18 that began Japan's occupation of Manchuria, to become Supreme Advisor to the Society for Maintaining Peace in Liaoning Province, based at Mukden (personnel record). Manchukuo, the Japanese-sponsored state that followed in March 1932, likewise has no dedicated entry in this atlas yet; see the project notes for that future cross-link candidate. From here his career ran through a sequence of Manchukuo provincial and investigative posts, per the same record: Chief of the General Affairs Office of Mukden Province in 1932; Director of the Special Investigation Committee on the System of Local Affairs in 1934; Chief of the General Affairs Office of Pinchiang Province from December 1935; and Provincial Governor of Antung from July 1936.

**A conflict in the sources, recorded not resolved:** the narrative summary of Kanai's own IMTFE defense testimony states that he was governor of Chientao (Jiandao, 間島省) Province in 1935. That posting appears nowhere in the same witness packet's personnel record, which moves directly from the Pinchiang post in December 1935 to the Antung governorship in July 1936. Both accounts stand here side by side. The personnel record's silence may mean the narrative summary is simply mistaken, or that the record omits a genuine but brief posting, or something else this atlas cannot determine from the material in hand. Neither account is preferred.

## Supreme Advisor to Mengjiang (the core of this entry)

**A second conflict in the sources, recorded not resolved:** three sources date the start of Kanai's Mengjiang advisorship differently, and none is harmonized here into a single settled date. Baidu Baike, a low-reliability Chinese-language aggregator framed throughout in explicit "puppet" and "pseudo-state" terms, places him as Japanese supreme advisor from the founding of the [Mongolian Allied League Autonomous Government](/entry/mongolian-allied-league-autonomous-government) in October 1937. The IMTFE personnel record instead lists "Supreme Advisor to the Mongolian Government, 1938." A Hoover Institution news release, a reputable institutional secondary source logged in this atlas's project notes with its full holdings lead, dates his role as supreme political advisor to the Mengjiang government to 1939 through 1941. The divergence plausibly tracks the Mongol government's own successive reorganizations in these years (the October 1937 Allied League government, the November 1937 [United Committee of Mengchiang](/entry/united-committee-of-mengchiang), and the September 1939 [Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government](/entry/mongolian-allied-autonomous-government)) rather than three sources describing one unchanging appointment. That reading is offered as plausible, not as a resolution. All three dates stand.

Within that broader span, one installation can be dated precisely. Kanai directed the September 1, 1939 founding ceremony of the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government in the courtyard of the United Committee of Mengchiang, and was installed as that government's Supreme Advisor (Jagchid). Per Boyle, Demchugdongrub had resigned that April as Director-General of the United Committee of Mengchiang in dissatisfaction with Japanese policy, and Kanai took over that role as well (Boyle, p. 133); this atlas's master chronology and the United Committee of Mengchiang entry treat these as the same office described by two different sources.

Kanai used his position to shape Mongol politics directly, not only administratively. He promoted the appointment of Prince Sungjingwangchug as Minister of Civilian Affairs, hoping to build up a rival who could eventually replace Demchugdongrub. Per Jagchid, who became Prince Sung's secretary, the plan backfired: Sung proved loyal, and Demchugdongrub came to rely on him as a trusted intermediary with the Japanese instead (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government entry). Kanai also arranged [Wu Heling](/entry/wu-heling)'s effective exile to Japan, under the pretext of deepening his knowledge of the country, removing a senior Mongol rival from Mengjiang politics. Jagchid, who accompanied Wu there from 1938 as his secretary, gives that episode in detail in the Wu Heling entry (Jagchid).

[Sechin Jagchid](/entry/sechin-jagchid) frames the broader relationship between the two men as an ongoing political battle that Demchugdongrub was losing: the concentration of Mengjiang's central institutions at [Kalgan](/entry/kalgan) from late 1937 onward "consolidated the authority of the Japanese supreme advisor Kanai Shoji at the prince's expense" (Jagchid; see the Kalgan entry).

## Decline, replacement, and departure

Kanai's dominance did not last through the whole Mengjiang period. Late in 1941, under the [Mongolian Autonomous State](/entry/mongolian-autonomous-state), he was removed and succeeded by Ohashi Chuichi, who himself went on to clash with the Japanese military over the state's autonomy (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Autonomous State entry). A visit to Kalgan that same year by the Japanese army minister Tojo Hideki, who presented Demchugdongrub with 500 rifles for a personal bodyguard, is recorded elsewhere in this atlas as a signal that Kanai no longer dominated Mongol affairs even before his formal removal (Jagchid; see the Mongolian Allied Autonomous Government entry). The IMTFE personnel record dates the retirement precisely, "retired from office by request, December 1941," sharpening what this atlas had previously recorded only as "late 1941."

Jagchid's memoir *The Last Mongol Prince*, written by a participant in this same milieu rather than a neutral outside historian (the standing caveat this atlas applies to all of Jagchid's material), includes in its illustrations list a photograph of a farewell party held for Kanai as the outgoing Japanese Supreme Advisor, in Beijing, in early December 1941. That matches the retirement date above. **Verify-gate:** the photograph listing surfaced via an online full text of uncertain legality. This atlas has not confirmed the exact page or the precise caption wording against a legitimate copy of the book, and that confirmation is left to the maintainer before this detail is treated as fully settled. No text from that source is reproduced here beyond the paraphrased fact of the photograph's existence and dating.

## After Mengjiang: the Race Research Institute and Tokyo Tribunal testimony

Kanai's postwar documentary trail resumes, per the IMTFE personnel record, with his appointment as Councillor of the Race Research Institute (民族研究所), a Cabinet appointment, on August 4, 1943. The two-year gap before it is one this atlas has no source to fill.

His final documented appearance in this atlas's sources is as a defense witness before the IMTFE in April 1947, in a witness packet held at the National Diet Library (scan NDL11918515). The packet contains four documents, treated here as four distinct voices rather than one: a defense narrative summary of his direct testimony (transcript pages 19219–19232); a prosecution memorandum dated April 17, 1947; a witness progress report; and the personnel record already cited above for his career dates. **This packet's romanizations of Chinese and Japanese names are unreliable and have been mapped to this atlas's standard forms before use here**; see the mapping table in the project notes.

The narrative summary is exculpatory defense testimony, and this entry treats it as such throughout, never as flat fact. It was solicited on Kanai's behalf by the Class-A defendants Itagaki Seishiro, Okawa Shumei, Minami Jiro, Umezu Yoshijiro, and Hoshino Naoki, men with a direct interest in favorable testimony from a witness who had served in the same colonial administrative apparatus. Most notably, Kanai testified that opium control under his administration was conducted rigorously and had the effect of reducing smoking. That claim is recorded here strictly as testimony, not as established fact; this atlas holds no independent source corroborating or contradicting it and does not evaluate it.

The prosecution's own memorandum is a separate and distinct voice from the defense narrative, and this atlas attributes it as such: it records the prosecution's judgment that Kanai's testimony was unimportant to its case. This atlas holds no further detail from that memorandum beyond that characterization. The witness progress report, the packet's fourth document, is logged here as part of the packet's provenance; nothing further is relayed from it.

IMTFE tribunal records are public domain, but this atlas paraphrases them rather than quoting at length, per house style.
